Isoflurane (Forane)

Quick Facts

  • Generic: Isoflurane
  • Brand: Forane
  • Class: Volatile halogenated inhaled anesthetic
  • Controlled substance: No
  • Last updated: 2026-04-04

Indication-Specific Guidance

1) Maintenance of General Anesthesia

Typical Dosing

  • MAC: 1.15% to 1.4% in adults

Practical Considerations

  • Most commonly used in cases where cost is a consideration (older, less expensive agent)
  • Slower onset/offset compared to sevoflurane and desflurane
  • Pungent odor — not suitable for inhalational induction

Onset and Duration

  • Blood/gas coefficient: 1.4 (higher than sevoflurane/desflurane — slower equilibration)
  • Longer emergence time than sevoflurane or desflurane

Mechanism of Action

  • Enhances GABA-A inhibitory neurotransmission and inhibits NMDA receptors

Contraindications / Avoid

  • Malignant hyperthermia (MH) susceptibility: Absolute contraindication
  • Mask induction: not recommended (pungent odor)
  • Known hypersensitivity to halogenated anesthetics

Key Warnings and Cautions

  • MH trigger
  • Coronary steal phenomenon: theoretical concern in patients with coronary artery disease (isoflurane may dilate coronary collaterals, shunting flow away from ischemic zones). Clinical significance debated.
  • Dose-dependent cardiovascular and respiratory depression

Common Adverse Effects

  • Hypotension
  • Respiratory depression
  • Airway irritation

Monitoring

  • Continuous end-tidal monitoring
  • Standard ASA monitors

Special Populations

  • MH susceptibility: absolutely contraindicated
  • Elderly: lower MAC requirement (reduce dose ~6% per decade)

Notes for Local Customization

  • Less commonly chosen in modern US practice; still widely used internationally and where cost is a factor.
