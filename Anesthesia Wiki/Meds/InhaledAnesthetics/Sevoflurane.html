Sevoflurane (Ultane)

Quick Facts

  • Generic: Sevoflurane
  • Brand: Ultane
  • Class: Volatile halogenated inhaled anesthetic
  • Controlled substance: No
  • Last updated: 2026-04-04

Indication-Specific Guidance

1) Maintenance of General Anesthesia

Typical Dosing

  • MAC: 1.8% to 2.0% (adjusted by age, adjuncts, clinical context)
  • 1 MAC = 2.0% in a 40-year-old; decreases ~6% per decade of age

Practical Considerations

  • Most commonly used volatile agent due to pleasant odor and low solubility
  • Rapid onset and offset; smooth emergence

2) Inhalational Induction (Mask Induction)

Typical Dosing

  • 6–8% for rapid mask induction, then titrate to maintenance

Practical Considerations

  • Drug of choice for pediatric inhalational induction due to non-pungent smell
  • Acceptable for adults with difficult IV access

Onset and Duration

  • Blood/gas coefficient: 0.65 (low — fast onset and offset)
  • Elimination: primarily pulmonary, minor hepatic metabolism (~3–5%)

Mechanism of Action

  • Enhances inhibitory GABA-A neurotransmission and inhibits excitatory NMDA receptors
  • Exact mechanism remains partially characterized

Contraindications / Avoid

  • Malignant hyperthermia (MH) susceptibility: Absolute contraindication — all volatile agents are MH triggers
  • Known hypersensitivity to halogenated anesthetics

Key Warnings and Cautions

  • MH trigger — have dantrolene immediately available in every anesthetizing location
  • Metabolism produces inorganic fluoride — at very high or prolonged doses, nephrotoxicity is theoretically possible (clinically rare in standard use)
  • Compound A: produced by reaction with CO₂ absorbents at low flows — avoid prolonged very low fresh gas flows (< 1 L/min) with desiccated absorbents
  • In obese patients, plasma fluoride concentrations may be significantly higher post-surgery

Common Adverse Effects

  • Bradycardia (dose-dependent)
  • Hypotension
  • Airway irritation (mild — lowest among volatile agents)
  • Breath-hold or coughing during induction (rare)

Monitoring

  • Continuous end-tidal monitoring required
  • Standard ASA monitors

Important Interactions

  • MAC reduced by: N₂O, opioids, benzodiazepines, age, hypothermia, dexmedetomidine
  • Mac increased by: pediatric age, hyperthermia, CNS stimulants

Special Populations

  • Pediatrics: agent of choice for mask induction
  • Obesity: monitor for elevated fluoride/longer emergence
  • MH susceptibility: absolutely contraindicated
