Desflurane (Suprane)

Quick Facts

  • Generic: Desflurane
  • Brand: Suprane
  • Class: Volatile halogenated inhaled anesthetic
  • Controlled substance: No
  • Last updated: 2026-04-04

Indication-Specific Guidance

1) Maintenance of General Anesthesia

Typical Dosing

  • MAC: 5.8% to 6.6% in adults (approximately 6% at 40 years of age)

Practical Considerations

  • Ultra-fast onset and offset — excellent for cases with rapid emergence goals
  • NOT suitable for mask induction (pungent odor causes airway irritation, breath-hold, coughing, laryngospasm)
  • Rapid emergence is its primary advantage in long ambulatory or neurosurgical cases

2) Prolonged or Neuro Cases Requiring Rapid Wake-Up

Typical Dosing

  • Titrate to MAC-adjusted target throughout case

Practical Considerations

  • Fastest offset of all volatile agents; useful when rapid neurologic assessment is needed post-op

Onset and Duration

  • Blood/gas coefficient: 0.42 (lowest of all volatile agents — ultra-fast equilibration)
  • Onset is very rapid; emergence similarly quick

Mechanism of Action

  • Enhances GABA-A inhibitory neurotransmission and inhibits NMDA receptors

Contraindications / Avoid

  • Malignant hyperthermia (MH) susceptibility: Absolute contraindication
  • Mask induction: Strong pungent odor causes coughing, breath-holding, laryngospasm, bronchospasm — do not use for induction
  • Known hypersensitivity to halogenated anesthetics

Key Warnings and Cautions

  • MH trigger — dantrolene must be available
  • Emergence delirium — higher incidence than sevoflurane
  • Bronchospasm risk if used in patients with reactive airway disease
  • Tachycardia: can cause sympathetic stimulation, especially with rapid increases in concentration
  • Special vaporizer required (heated, pressurized) — not standard temperature-compensated vaporizer
  • Environmental concerns: high global warming potential

Common Adverse Effects

  • Tachycardia with rapid increases in concentration
  • Airway irritation / bronchospasm
  • Emergence delirium (more common than sevoflurane)
  • Hypotension (dose-dependent)

Monitoring

  • Continuous end-tidal monitoring required
  • Heart rate monitoring — watch for tachycardia with concentration changes

Important Interactions

  • MAC reduced by: N₂O, opioids, benzodiazepines, age, dexmedetomidine
  • Increase concentration gradually to avoid tachycardia

Special Populations

  • Pediatrics: NOT recommended for induction; same MAC principles apply for maintenance
  • Reactive airway disease: use with caution; consider sevoflurane instead

Notes for Local Customization

  • Desflurane is being phased out in some institutions due to environmental concerns.
