# Droperidol

## Quick Facts
- Generic: Droperidol
- Brand: Inapsine (legacy)
- Class: Butyrophenone antipsychotic / antiemetic (D2 antagonist)
- Controlled substance: No
- Last updated: 2026-04-04

## Indication-Specific Guidance

### 1) PONV Prophylaxis / Treatment
#### Dosing
- 0.625 to 1.25 mg IV at end of case or in PACU
#### Practical Considerations
- Very effective antiemetic at low dose
- Often used when multimodal prophylaxis is needed

### 2) Agitation (selected settings)
#### Dosing
- Institution-specific, typically higher than antiemetic dose

## Onset and Duration
- Onset: 3–10 minutes IV
- Duration: 2–6 hours

## Mechanism of Action
- Dopamine D2 receptor antagonism in chemoreceptor trigger zone
- Additional alpha-adrenergic blockade contributes to hypotension risk

## Contraindications / Avoid
- Known QT prolongation or history of torsades de pointes
- Significant bradycardia, uncompensated electrolyte abnormalities
- Hypersensitivity

## Key Warnings and Cautions
- **Black box warning: QT prolongation and torsades risk**
- Avoid with other QT-prolonging drugs when possible
- Use lowest effective antiemetic dose and monitor in higher-risk patients

## Common Adverse Effects
- Sedation
- Hypotension
- Extrapyramidal symptoms (rare at antiemetic doses)

## Monitoring
- ECG/QT assessment in high-risk patients
- Hemodynamics after administration

## Important Interactions
- Additive QT prolongation with other QT-prolonging medications
- Increased sedation with other CNS depressants

## Notes for Local Customization
- Low-dose droperidol remains highly effective for refractory PONV when used judiciously.
