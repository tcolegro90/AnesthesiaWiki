# Adenosine

## Quick Facts
- Generic: Adenosine
- Brand: Adenocard
- Class: Endogenous nucleoside antiarrhythmic (AV nodal blocker)
- Controlled substance: No
- Last updated: 2026-04-04

## Indication-Specific Guidance

### 1) Stable, Regular Narrow-Complex SVT
#### Dosing
- 6 mg rapid IV push + immediate flush
- If no conversion in 1–2 minutes, 12 mg rapid IV push
- May repeat 12 mg once per protocol
#### Practical Considerations
- Use proximal IV and rapid saline flush
- Inform awake patient of brief chest pressure/flushing sensation

## Onset and Duration
- Onset: seconds
- Half-life: < 10 seconds
- Duration: very brief

## Mechanism of Action
- Activates A1 receptors in AV node, transiently blocking AV nodal conduction

## Contraindications / Avoid
- 2nd/3rd degree AV block or sick sinus syndrome without pacing
- Irregular/wide-complex tachycardias where diagnosis is uncertain
- Asthma with active bronchospasm (relative)

## Key Warnings and Cautions
- Brief asystole is expected
- Can provoke bronchospasm in susceptible patients
- In pre-excited atrial fibrillation, can worsen ventricular response

## Common Adverse Effects
- Flushing
- Chest discomfort
- Dyspnea
- Sense of impending doom (transient)

## Monitoring
- Continuous ECG during administration
- Resuscitation readiness for unstable rhythms

## Important Interactions
- Theophylline/caffeine antagonize effect (may require larger doses)
- Dipyridamole potentiates effect (consider lower dose)

## Notes for Local Customization
- Best for regular monomorphic narrow-complex tachycardia likely to be AV node dependent.
