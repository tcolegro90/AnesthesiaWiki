# Esmolol

## Quick Facts
- Generic: Esmolol
- Brand: Brevibloc
- Class: Ultra-short acting beta-1 selective blocker
- Controlled substance: No
- Last updated: 2026-04-04

## Indication-Specific Guidance

### 1) Perioperative Tachycardia / Hypertension
#### Dosing
- Bolus: 10 to 50 mg IV (or 0.5 mg/kg)
- Infusion: 50 to 300 mcg/kg/min
#### Practical Considerations
- Very short half-life allows rapid titration and quick discontinuation

### 2) Rate Control in SVT/Afib with RVR (selected cases)
#### Dosing
- Institutional protocol-based loading + infusion

## Onset and Duration
- Onset: 1–2 minutes
- Half-life: about 9 minutes
- Duration: 10–20 minutes after stopping

## Mechanism of Action
- Competitive beta-1 antagonism decreases HR, AV nodal conduction, and myocardial oxygen demand

## Contraindications / Avoid
- Severe bradycardia
- 2nd/3rd degree AV block without pacing
- Decompensated heart failure/cardiogenic shock

## Key Warnings and Cautions
- Can precipitate hypotension and bradycardia rapidly
- Use caution in reactive airway disease (beta-1 selective but not absolute)

## Common Adverse Effects
- Bradycardia
- Hypotension
- Fatigue

## Monitoring
- Continuous ECG and BP

## Important Interactions
- Additive AV nodal suppression with calcium channel blockers/digoxin

## Notes for Local Customization
- Useful when temporary beta blockade is desired because effects wash out quickly.
