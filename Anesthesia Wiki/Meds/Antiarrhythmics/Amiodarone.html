Amiodarone

Quick Facts

  • Generic: Amiodarone
  • Brand: Cordarone, Nexterone
  • Class: Class III antiarrhythmic with multi-class effects
  • Controlled substance: No
  • Last updated: 2026-04-04

Indication-Specific Guidance

1) Ventricular Arrhythmias (ACLS contexts)

Dosing

  • Pulseless VT/VF: 300 mg IV/IO bolus, then 150 mg if needed
  • Stable wide-complex tachycardia: 150 mg IV over 10 minutes (institution-dependent)

2) Atrial Fibrillation/Flutter Rhythm Control (selected settings)

Dosing

  • Loading + infusion protocols vary

Onset and Duration

  • Onset: minutes IV
  • Terminal half-life: very long with chronic therapy (weeks)

Mechanism of Action

  • Predominantly potassium channel blockade (class III) with additional sodium channel, beta-blocking, and calcium channel effects

Contraindications / Avoid

  • Severe sinus node dysfunction or AV block without pacing
  • Iodine hypersensitivity (relative nuance by formulation)

Key Warnings and Cautions

  • Hypotension and bradycardia can occur with IV administration
  • Chronic therapy has pulmonary, thyroid, hepatic, ocular toxicities

Common Adverse Effects

  • Bradycardia
  • Hypotension (especially rapid IV infusion)
  • QT prolongation (torsades less common than with many class III agents)

Monitoring

  • ECG/QT, blood pressure during infusion
  • Long-term: thyroid, liver, pulmonary monitoring

Important Interactions

  • Increases levels/effects of warfarin and digoxin
  • Additive QT effects with other QT-prolonging drugs

Notes for Local Customization

  • Widely used in peri-arrest and ICU arrhythmia pathways.
