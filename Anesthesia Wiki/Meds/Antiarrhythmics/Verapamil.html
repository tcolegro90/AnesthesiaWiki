Verapamil

Quick Facts

  • Generic: Verapamil
  • Brand: Calan, Isoptin
  • Class: Non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker
  • Controlled substance: No
  • Last updated: 2026-04-04

Indication-Specific Guidance

1) SVT Termination / Rate Control (selected cases)

Dosing

  • 2.5 to 5 mg IV over 2 minutes
  • Additional 5 to 10 mg after 15–30 minutes if needed

Practical Considerations

  • Alternative when adenosine is ineffective/contraindicated (context-dependent)

Onset and Duration

  • Onset: 1–5 minutes IV
  • Duration: 30–60 minutes

Mechanism of Action

  • L-type calcium channel blockade slows AV nodal conduction and decreases contractility

Contraindications / Avoid

  • Hypotension
  • Decompensated heart failure with reduced EF
  • AV block without pacing
  • Wide-complex tachycardia of uncertain origin
  • WPW with atrial fibrillation

Key Warnings and Cautions

  • Can cause profound hypotension/bradycardia
  • Negative inotropy may worsen cardiogenic states

Common Adverse Effects

  • Hypotension
  • Bradycardia
  • AV block

Monitoring

  • Continuous ECG and BP during IV use

Important Interactions

  • Additive bradycardia/AV block with beta blockers, digoxin

Notes for Local Customization

  • Use carefully in anesthesia settings where hemodynamics may already be tenuous.
