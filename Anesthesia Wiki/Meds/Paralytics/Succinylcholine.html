Succinylcholine (Anectine)

Quick Facts

  • Generic: Succinylcholine chloride
  • Brand: Anectine, Quelicin
  • Class: Depolarizing neuromuscular blocking agent (NMBA)
  • Controlled substance: No
  • Last updated: 2026-04-04

Indication-Specific Guidance

1) Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI)

Typical Dosing

  • 1 to 1.5 mg/kg IV (standard dose)
  • Up to 2 mg/kg for true RSI in morbidly obese (dose to total body weight)

Practical Considerations

  • Fastest onset and offset of any NMBA — creates intubating conditions in ~60 seconds
  • Gold standard for RSI when rapid return of spontaneous ventilation matters

2) Laryngospasm Treatment

Typical Dosing

  • 0.1 to 0.2 mg/kg IV (partial dose); 4 mg/kg IM if no IV access

Practical Considerations

  • Small IV bolus can break laryngospasm rapidly
  • IM route (with higher dose) effective when IV access is unavailable

Onset and Duration

  • Onset IV: 45 to 60 seconds
  • Duration: 8 to 12 minutes (metabolized by plasma pseudocholinesterase)

Mechanism of Action

  • Depolarizing NMBA: binds nicotinic acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction, causing persistent depolarization (Phase I block)
  • Produces fasciculations followed by flaccid paralysis

Contraindications / Avoid

  • Malignant hyperthermia susceptibility: Absolute contraindication — potent MH trigger
  • Muscular dystrophies / myopathies: Risk of rhabdomyolysis, severe hyperkalemia, and cardiac arrest
  • Burns (after 24–48 hours through healing): Up-regulation of extrajunctional AChRs causes massive K⁺ release → life-threatening hyperkalemia
  • Crush injuries / prolonged immobility: Same K⁺ mechanism
  • Pseudocholinesterase deficiency: Prolonged neuromuscular blockade (hours)
  • Denervation injuries: Upper/lower motor neuron lesions cause receptor up-regulation → hyperkalemia risk
  • Personal or family history of MH
  • Known hypersensitivity

Key Warnings and Cautions

  • Hyperkalemia: serum K⁺ rises ~0.5 mEq/L normally; can be catastrophic in high-risk patients
  • Fasciculations: can cause myalgias post-operatively; pretreatment with small dose of NDMR can reduce but add complexity
  • Increased intragastric pressure (offset by increased lower esophageal sphincter tone)
  • Bradycardia / sinus arrest — especially in children; second doses carry high risk → pretreat with atropine

Common Adverse Effects

  • Fasciculations
  • Post-operative myalgias
  • Bradycardia (especially with repeat doses)
  • Hyperkalemia (in susceptible patients)
  • Increased intraocular pressure

Monitoring

  • SpO₂, ECG — especially intra-induction
  • Return of neuromuscular function (twitch monitor)

Reversal / Overdose Strategy

  • No direct reversal agent in standard Phase I block
  • Sugammadex does NOT reverse succinylcholine
  • Pseudocholinesterase deficiency: Phase II block may partially respond to neostigmine; primarily supportive

Special Populations

  • Pediatrics: use with caution; risk of cardiac arrest from undiagnosed myopathies — consider routine use only for true RSI or laryngospasm
  • Pregnancy: pseudocholinesterase activity decreases; may have slightly prolonged duration

Notes for Local Customization

  • Many institutions require documented indication for succinylcholine use. Rocuronium + sugammadex is an alternative RSI strategy.
