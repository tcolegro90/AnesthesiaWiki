Rocuronium (Zemuron)

Quick Facts

  • Generic: Rocuronium bromide
  • Brand: Zemuron
  • Class: Nondepolarizing neuromuscular blocking agent (NDMRA; aminosteroid)
  • Controlled substance: No
  • Last updated: 2026-04-04

Indication-Specific Guidance

1) Facilitation of Intubation (Standard)

Typical Dosing

  • 0.6 mg/kg IV — intubating conditions in ~90 seconds; duration ~30–45 min

Practical Considerations

  • Most commonly used NDMRA for routine intubation

2) Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI Alternative to Succinylcholine)

Typical Dosing

  • 1.2 mg/kg IV — intubating conditions in ~60 seconds; duration significantly prolonged (~60–90+ min)
  • Must plan for sugammadex reversal if high-dose rocuronium is used

Practical Considerations

  • Preferred over succinylcholine when succinylcholine is contraindicated
  • Sugammadex (16 mg/kg) provides immediate reversal even at high-dose RSI doses

3) Maintenance Relaxation

Typical Dosing

  • 0.1 to 0.2 mg/kg supplemental boluses, guided by train-of-four monitoring

Practical Considerations

  • Titrate to surgical need; avoid over-paralysis

Onset and Duration

  • Standard dose (0.6 mg/kg): onset ~90 seconds, duration ~30–45 minutes
  • High dose (1.2 mg/kg): onset ~60 seconds, duration ~60–90 minutes

Mechanism of Action

  • Competitive antagonist at nicotinic acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction (nondepolarizing)
  • No fasciculations; no depolarization

Contraindications / Avoid

  • Known hypersensitivity to rocuronium

Key Warnings and Cautions

  • Cannot be reversed with neostigmine if block is deep (TOF count 0–1); must use sugammadex or wait
  • Fully reversible with sugammadex (only NDMRA with a specific reversal agent available for deep block)
  • Prolonged duration in hepatic disease (liver is primary elimination route)
  • Renal disease: prolonged recovery in severe renal failure

Common Adverse Effects

  • Prolonged blockade if reversal not given or misjudged
  • Anaphylaxis (rare but rocuronium has one of the highest rates among NMBAs)

Monitoring

  • Train-of-four (TOF) neuromuscular monitoring required for safe dosing and reversal

Important Interactions

  • Volatile anesthetics: potentiate block (shift dose-response curve)
  • Aminoglycosides, magnesium, calcium channel blockers: may prolong block
  • Hypothermia: prolongs duration

Reversal / Overdose Strategy

  • Neostigmine: effective at moderate block (TOF ≥ 2 twitches); give with glycopyrrolate
  • Sugammadex: works at any depth of block — preferred; see sugammadex dosing guide

Special Populations

  • Hepatic impairment: prolonged duration — reduce maintenance doses and monitor TOF closely
  • Obesity: dose to IBW for standard intubation dosing; some institutions use LBW

Notes for Local Customization

  • Sugammadex availability should be confirmed before using high-dose RSI rocuronium.
