# Atracurium

## Quick Facts
- Generic: Atracurium besylate
- Class: Nondepolarizing neuromuscular blocking agent (NDMRA; benzylisoquinolinium)
- Controlled substance: No
- Last updated: 2026-04-04

## Indication-Specific Guidance

### 1) Facilitation of Intubation
#### Typical Dosing
- 0.4 to 0.5 mg/kg IV
#### Practical Considerations
- Intermediate onset (~3–5 min); less commonly chosen than rocuronium or cisatracurium in modern practice

### 2) Maintenance Relaxation
#### Typical Dosing
- 0.1 mg/kg supplemental boluses, guided by TOF
#### Practical Considerations
- Intermediate duration makes it suitable for moderate-length procedures
- Organ-independent elimination via Hofmann degradation and ester hydrolysis

## Onset and Duration
- Onset: 3 to 5 minutes
- Duration: 25 to 40 minutes

## Mechanism of Action
- Competitive antagonist at nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nondepolarizing)
- Undergoes Hofmann degradation and ester hydrolysis — independent of hepatic and renal function

## Contraindications / Avoid
- Known hypersensitivity

## Key Warnings and Cautions
- **Histamine release**: Dose-dependent; can cause flushing, bronchospasm, and hypotension. More histamine release than cisatracurium at equivalent doses. Inject slowly and consider antihistamine pretreatment in susceptible patients.
- **Laudanosine**: A metabolite of Hofmann degradation; at very high cumulative doses may cause CNS excitation (theoretical concern with prolonged infusions)
- Reflex tachycardia can occur secondary to histamine-mediated vasodilation
- Cannot be reversed with sugammadex

## Common Adverse Effects
- Flushing
- Hypotension
- Bronchospasm (histamine mediated)
- Reflex tachycardia

## Monitoring
- TOF monitoring required
- Blood pressure — expect mild hypotension from histamine release

## Reversal / Overdose Strategy
- **Neostigmine** + glycopyrrolate (at adequate recovery)
- Sugammadex: **NOT effective**

## Special Populations
- Renal/hepatic failure: acceptable choice due to organ-independent elimination
- Asthma / reactive airway: use with caution (histamine release risk); cisatracurium preferred

## Notes for Local Customization
- Cisatracurium is generally preferred over atracurium due to its lack of histamine release.
