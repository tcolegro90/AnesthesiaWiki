Vecuronium

Quick Facts

  • Generic: Vecuronium bromide
  • Brand: Norcuron
  • Class: Nondepolarizing neuromuscular blocking agent (NDMRA; aminosteroid)
  • Controlled substance: No
  • Last updated: 2026-04-04

Indication-Specific Guidance

1) Facilitation of Intubation

Typical Dosing

  • 0.1 mg/kg IV — onset ~3–5 minutes

Practical Considerations

  • Not ideal for RSI due to slow onset; rocuronium preferred
  • Good intermediate agent for standard intubation when rocuronium is not available

2) Maintenance Relaxation

Typical Dosing

  • 0.01 to 0.015 mg/kg supplemental boluses guided by TOF

Practical Considerations

  • Intermediate duration suitable for most OR cases

Onset and Duration

  • Onset: 3 to 5 minutes
  • Duration: 25 to 40 minutes

Mechanism of Action

  • Competitive antagonist at nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nondepolarizing)
  • Aminosteroid class — structurally related to rocuronium, pancuronium (ring steroid)

Contraindications / Avoid

  • Known hypersensitivity

Key Warnings and Cautions

  • Hepatic disease: Liver is the primary route of elimination — duration of action is significantly prolonged in patients with hepatic disease or cirrhosis. Monitor TOF closely.
  • Renal disease: Mild prolongation; less significant than hepatic disease
  • No histamine release and no cardiovascular effects at standard doses (advantage over atracurium)

Common Adverse Effects

  • Prolonged block in hepatic disease
  • Residual weakness post-operatively if reversal is inadequate

Monitoring

  • TOF monitoring required
  • Assess train-of-four before extubation

Important Interactions

  • Volatile anesthetics: potentiate block
  • Aminoglycosides, magnesium, calcium channel blockers: prolong block
  • Hypothermia: prolongs duration

Reversal / Overdose Strategy

  • Neostigmine + glycopyrrolate: effective at moderate block
  • Sugammadex: vecuronium is an aminosteroid — can be reversed with sugammadex (same binding mechanism as rocuronium)

Special Populations

  • Hepatic failure: use with caution; consider cisatracurium instead
  • Renal failure: mild prolongation; generally acceptable with monitoring

Notes for Local Customization

  • Less commonly used than rocuronium in most modern US centers due to slower onset.
