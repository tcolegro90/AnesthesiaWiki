# Etomidate

## Quick Facts
- Generic: Etomidate
- Brand: Amidate
- Class: Intravenous anesthetic (imidazole-based)
- Controlled substance: No
- Last updated: 2026-04-04

## Indication-Specific Guidance

### 1) Induction of General Anesthesia
#### Typical Dosing
- 0.2 to 0.3 mg/kg IV
#### Practical Considerations
- Drug of choice when hemodynamic stability is critical (e.g., trauma, hemorrhage, cardiomyopathy)
- Causes only ~2% change in blood pressure on induction
- Pretreat with fentanyl or lidocaine to reduce myoclonus

### 2) RSI Induction (Hemodynamically Unstable Patient)
#### Typical Dosing
- Same dose as standard induction (0.3 mg/kg)
#### Practical Considerations
- Paired with succinylcholine or rocuronium for rapid sequence
- Preferred over propofol in the unstable patient

## Onset and Duration
- Onset: 1 to 3 minutes
- Peak effect: about 1 minute
- Duration: 15 to 30 minutes
- Context-sensitive half-time (CST 1/2): ~5 min at 2 hr, ~10 min at 4 hr, ~20 min at 6 hr

## Mechanism of Action
- GABA-A receptor agonist (imidazole ring interacts at benzodiazepine site)

## Contraindications / Avoid
- Known hypersensitivity
- Prolonged infusions (not indicated — adrenal suppression risk)

## Key Warnings and Cautions
- **Adrenal suppression**: A single induction dose can suppress cortisol production for 6–12 hours; repeated doses or infusions cause prolonged suppression. Use with caution in critically ill patients where endogenous cortisol stress response is important.
- **Myoclonus**: Common (up to 30–60%); does not indicate seizure activity. Pretreatment with opioids or benzodiazepines reduces incidence.
- **PONV**: Higher incidence compared to propofol.
- **Pain on injection**: Inject into a large, freely flowing IV.

## Common Adverse Effects
- Myoclonus
- PONV
- Adrenal suppression
- Pain on injection

## Monitoring
- Blood pressure, heart rate (minimal change expected)
- Watch for myoclonus at induction

## Important Interactions
- Opioid pretreatment reduces myoclonus and blunts laryngoscopy response

## Preparation and Administration Notes
- Comes as 2 mg/mL solution
- Inject over 30 to 60 seconds

## Reversal / Overdose Strategy
- No direct reversal agent
- Support airway and ventilation

## Special Populations
- Elderly/frail: excellent choice due to hemodynamic stability
- Septic shock: weigh benefit/risk given adrenal suppression concern

## Notes for Local Customization
- Some institutions supplement with hydrocortisone post-etomidate in critically ill patients.
