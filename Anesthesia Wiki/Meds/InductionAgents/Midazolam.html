Midazolam (Versed)

Quick Facts

  • Generic: Midazolam
  • Brand: Versed
  • Class: Benzodiazepine
  • Controlled substance: Yes (Schedule IV in the US)
  • Last updated: 2026-04-04

Indication-Specific Guidance

1) Preoperative Anxiolysis / Amnesia

Typical Dosing

  • 1 to 2 mg IV (titrate, especially in elderly)

Practical Considerations

  • Provides reliable pre-induction amnesia and anxiolysis
  • Minimal hangover at low doses; higher doses may prolong emergence

2) Reduction of Ketamine Emergence Reactions

Typical Dosing

  • 0.5 to 2 mg IV prior to or with ketamine

Practical Considerations

  • Co-administration significantly reduces emergence delirium and hallucinations

3) Seizure Management (Intraoperative)

Typical Dosing

  • 1 to 2 mg IV increments, titrated

Practical Considerations

  • Preferred benzodiazepine for intraoperative seizures when propofol is not ideal

4) ICU Sedation (Acute/Short-Term)

Typical Dosing

  • Institution-specific continuous infusion

Practical Considerations

  • Prolonged infusions can cause significant accumulation and delayed awakening

Onset and Duration

  • Onset IV: 2 to 3 minutes
  • Duration: 30 to 60 minutes (single dose); prolonged with repeated doses or infusions

Mechanism of Action

  • Positive allosteric modulator of GABA-A receptors (benzodiazepine binding site)

Contraindications / Avoid

  • Known hypersensitivity
  • Acute angle-closure glaucoma

Key Warnings and Cautions

  • Prolonged emergence: High doses or prolonged infusions can delay awakening, especially in elderly patients
  • Respiratory depression — additive with opioids; monitor closely
  • Anterograde amnesia is a desired effect but should be anticipated by the patient
  • Paradoxical agitation can occur, particularly in elderly and pediatric patients

Common Adverse Effects

  • Sedation / prolonged drowsiness
  • Respiratory depression
  • Anterograde amnesia
  • Paradoxical agitation (uncommon)

Monitoring

  • Respiratory rate, SpO2
  • Level of sedation

Important Interactions

  • Additive CNS/respiratory depression with opioids, propofol, volatile anesthetics
  • CYP3A4 inhibitors can prolong duration significantly

Reversal / Overdose Strategy

  • Flumazenil: 0.2 mg IV, repeat every 1 min up to 1 mg total
  • Short duration of flumazenil — re-sedation is possible; monitor for resedation

Special Populations

  • Elderly: Use lowest effective dose; prolonged half-life and sensitivity increase
  • Hepatic impairment: Prolonged elimination

Notes for Local Customization

  • Institutional protocols vary on routine pre-medication dosing.
