# Dexmedetomidine (Precedex)

## Quick Facts
- Generic: Dexmedetomidine
- Brand: Precedex
- Class: Alpha-2 adrenergic agonist / sedative-analgesic
- Controlled substance: No
- Last updated: 2026-04-04

## Indication-Specific Guidance

### 1) Procedural Sedation / MAC Adjunct
#### Typical Dosing
- Loading dose: 0.5 to 1 mcg/kg over 10 minutes, then 0.2 to 0.7 mcg/kg/hr
#### Practical Considerations
- Produces cooperative, arousable sedation while maintaining spontaneous ventilation
- Useful for awake intubation and fiberoptic procedures

### 2) ICU Sedation
#### Typical Dosing
- 0.2 to 0.7 mcg/kg/hr infusion, titrated to goal sedation score
#### Practical Considerations
- Reduces opioid requirements
- Patients can be easily aroused and follow commands

### 3) Adjunct for Blunting Sympathetic Response / Opioid Sparing
#### Typical Dosing
- Small boluses or low-dose infusion throughout anesthetic
#### Practical Considerations
- Reduces intraoperative opioid requirements and can decrease MAC of volatile agents

## Onset and Duration
- Onset IV: 5 to 10 minutes
- Duration: dose and context dependent

## Mechanism of Action
- Highly selective Alpha-2 adrenergic receptor agonist
- Decreases norepinephrine release → sedation, analgesia, anxiolysis without respiratory depression

## Contraindications / Avoid
- Advanced heart block without pacemaker (relative)
- Severe bradycardia or hypotension

## Key Warnings and Cautions
- **Bradycardia and hypotension**: Common, especially with loading dose; administer slowly
- Despite being a sedative, does NOT cause significant respiratory depression — key advantage
- Transient hypertension can occur during rapid loading dose (alpha-2B activation in vasculature)

## Common Adverse Effects
- Bradycardia
- Hypotension
- Dry mouth
- Transient hypertension with rapid loading

## Monitoring
- Heart rate and blood pressure throughout infusion
- Sedation score

## Important Interactions
- Additive hypotension with volatile anesthetics and antihypertensives
- May potentiate effects of other sedatives; reduce co-administered doses

## Preparation and Administration Notes
- Dilute to 4 mcg/mL for IV infusion
- Loading dose should be given over at least 10 minutes to reduce hemodynamic effects

## Reversal / Overdose Strategy
- No direct reversal agent
- Reduce/stop infusion; treat bradycardia with atropine; treat hypotension with vasopressors as needed

## Special Populations
- Elderly: increased sensitivity to bradycardia and hypotension
- Hepatic impairment: prolonged duration

## Notes for Local Customization
- Institutional practice varies on routine use of loading dose.
