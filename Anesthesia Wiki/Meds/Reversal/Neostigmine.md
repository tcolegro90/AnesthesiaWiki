# Neostigmine

## Quick Facts
- Generic: Neostigmine methylsulfate
- Brand: Bloxiverz (and others)
- Class: Anticholinesterase / NMBA reversal agent
- Controlled substance: No
- Last updated: 2026-04-04

## Indication-Specific Guidance

### 1) Reversal of Nondepolarizing Neuromuscular Blockade
#### Typical Dosing
- 0.04 to 0.08 mg/kg IV; Maximum dose: 5 mg
- Always combined with glycopyrrolate: 0.2 mg glycopyrrolate per 1 mg neostigmine
#### Practical Considerations
- MUST be given with an anticholinergic (glycopyrrolate preferred) to prevent severe bradycardia and other muscarinic side effects
- Effective only when **adequate spontaneous recovery has started** (ideally TOF ≥ 2 twitches / T4:T1 ratio > 0.1)
- Cannot reverse deep block (TOF count 0–1) — requires waiting for spontaneous recovery or use of sugammadex

## Onset and Duration
- Onset: 5 to 15 minutes
- Duration: 45 to 90 minutes

## Mechanism of Action
- Anticholinesterase — inhibits acetylcholinesterase at the neuromuscular junction, allowing acetylcholine to accumulate and competitively displace nondepolarizing NMBAs

## Contraindications / Avoid
- Deep neuromuscular blockade (TOF = 0) — will not effectively reverse a full block and causes excessive muscarinic side effects
- Urinary or gastrointestinal obstruction (relative — anticholinergic offset required)
- Never use without an anticholinergic agent

## Key Warnings and Cautions
- **Cholinergic crisis if given without anticholinergic**: bradycardia, bronchospasm, bronchorrhea, increased secretions, bowel cramping
- Do NOT use to reverse succinylcholine (different mechanism; Phase II block only with careful assessment)
- "Reversal gap": neostigmine's effect can wear off before the NMBA does if block was not adequately recovered — residual blockade can recur

## Common Adverse Effects
- Bradycardia (if administered without anticholinergic)
- Increased salivation and secretions
- Nausea / vomiting
- Abdominal cramping

## Monitoring
- TOF ratio ideally > 0.9 before extubation (not simply ≥ 2 twitches)
- Heart rate monitoring during administration

## Important Interactions
- **Glycopyrrolate**: co-administration required to block muscarinic effects
- **Atropine**: alternative anticholinergic; faster onset but more central effects

## Reversal / Overdose Strategy
- Cholinergic excess: treat with atropine

## Special Populations
- Renal impairment: elimination prolonged; may be advantageous or cause excess duration

## Notes for Local Customization
- Sugammadex is increasingly used as the preferred reversal agent for aminosteroid NMBAs (rocuronium, vecuronium) due to superior reliability and ability to reverse deep block.
