Edrophonium (Enlon)

Quick Facts

  • Generic: Edrophonium chloride
  • Brand: Enlon, Tensilon
  • Class: Anticholinesterase / NMBA reversal agent
  • Controlled substance: No
  • Last updated: 2026-04-04

Indication-Specific Guidance

1) Reversal of Nondepolarizing Neuromuscular Blockade (Mild/Moderate)

Typical Dosing

  • 0.5 to 1 mg/kg IV; Maximum dose: 1 mg/kg (500–1000 mcg/kg)
  • Must be combined with atropine (due to rapid onset)

Practical Considerations

  • Rapid onset; shorter duration than neostigmine
  • NOT recommended for deep block (TOF = 0)
  • Less commonly used in modern anesthesia; largely replaced by neostigmine and sugammadex

2) Diagnosis of Myasthenia Gravis (Tensilon Test)

Typical Dosing

  • 2 mg IV test dose; if tolerated, give 8 mg IV

Practical Considerations

  • A positive test (transient improvement in muscle strength) suggests myasthenia gravis
  • Performed under resuscitation-ready conditions — risk of cholinergic crisis

Onset and Duration

  • Onset: 1 to 2 minutes (faster than neostigmine)
  • Duration: 10 to 20 minutes (shorter than neostigmine — must plan for recurarization)

Mechanism of Action

  • Reversible competitive inhibitor of acetylcholinesterase at the neuromuscular junction
  • Shorter acting than neostigmine due to electrostatic attachment rather than covalent binding

Contraindications / Avoid

  • Deep neuromuscular blockade
  • Combined without anticholinergic (dangerous bradycardia)
  • Mechanical urinary or GI obstruction

Key Warnings and Cautions

  • Rapid onset of muscarinic effects: Must co-administer atropine (not glycopyrrolate — atropine's faster onset matches edrophonium)
  • Short duration means recurarization can occur once effect wears off
  • Potential cholinergic crisis if given at inadequate block depth

Common Adverse Effects

  • Bradycardia (if anticholinergic not given)
  • Increased secretions
  • Nausea

Monitoring

  • TOF before administration
  • Heart rate — monitor during and after

Important Interactions

  • Atropine required: 10 mcg/kg atropine given with edrophonium due to rapid onset of action

Notes for Local Customization

  • Largely replaced by neostigmine + glycopyrrolate and sugammadex in modern practice. Still used for myasthenia testing.
