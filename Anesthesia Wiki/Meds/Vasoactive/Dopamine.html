Dopamine

Quick Facts

  • Generic: Dopamine
  • Brand: Intropin (legacy)
  • Class: Dose-dependent catecholamine inotrope/vasopressor
  • Controlled substance: No
  • Last updated: 2026-04-04

Indication-Specific Guidance

1) Selected Shock States (now less commonly first-line)

Dosing

  • 2 to 20 mcg/kg/min IV infusion

Practical Considerations

  • Historically used for bradycardic hypotension and low-output states
  • Often replaced by norepinephrine-based strategies due to arrhythmia risk

Onset and Duration

  • Onset: rapid (minutes)
  • Duration: short, titratable infusion effect

Mechanism of Action

  • Dose-dependent receptor activity:
  • Lower doses: dopaminergic/limited beta effects
  • Moderate doses: beta-1 inotropy/chronotropy
  • Higher doses: alpha-mediated vasoconstriction

Contraindications / Avoid

  • Pheochromocytoma
  • Uncorrected tachyarrhythmias (relative)

Key Warnings and Cautions

  • Higher arrhythmia burden than norepinephrine in many critical care populations
  • Extravasation can cause ischemia

Common Adverse Effects

  • Tachycardia
  • Arrhythmias
  • Hypertension

Monitoring

  • Continuous ECG and BP
  • Perfusion and urine output trends

Important Interactions

  • Additive sympathomimetic effects with other catecholamines

Notes for Local Customization

  • Consider specific institutional protocols and evolving evidence when selecting dopamine.
