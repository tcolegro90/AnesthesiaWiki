Propofol

Quick Facts

  • Generic: Propofol
  • Brand: Diprivan
  • Class: Intravenous anesthetic (sedative-hypnotic)
  • Controlled substance: No
  • Last updated: 2026-04-04

Indication-Specific Guidance

1) Induction of General Anesthesia

Typical Dosing

  • Healthy adults: 1.5 to 2.5 mg/kg IV bolus
  • Elderly/frail/hypovolemic: usually lower dose requirement

Practical Considerations

  • Expect hypotension and apnea, especially with rapid bolus
  • Pretreat/prepare for hemodynamic support when needed
  • Injection pain mitigation may be useful per local practice

2) Maintenance of Anesthesia (TIVA)

Typical Dosing

  • Continuous infusion: often about 50 to 200 mcg/kg/min
  • Titrate to anesthetic depth and hemodynamic tolerance

Practical Considerations

  • Lower requirements when combined with opioids/adjuncts
  • Continuous close BP/ventilation monitoring required
  • Watch cumulative dose and infusion duration

3) PONV Risk-Reduction Strategy (Adjunct Context)

Typical Dosing Context

  • Adjunct antiemetic bolus: 10 to 30 mg IV (institution-dependent)
  • Also commonly used as part of TIVA strategy to reduce PONV versus volatile-heavy plans

Practical Considerations

  • For antiemetic adjunct use, small bolus dosing (10 to 30 mg IV) may be used near emergence or in high-risk settings per local protocol
  • Coordinate with antiemetics (ondansetron, dexamethasone, etc.) based on risk profile

4) Procedural Sedation

Typical Dosing

  • Initial bolus: 0.5 to 1 mg/kg IV, then small titrated boluses/infusion

Practical Considerations

  • Apnea/hypoventilation risk increases with co-administered opioids/benzos
  • Titrate slowly to avoid oversedation and hypotension

5) ICU Sedation (Ventilated Patients)

Typical Dosing

  • Institution-specific infusion protocols (often lower range start, titrate to sedation goal)

Practical Considerations

  • Monitor triglycerides and prolonged infusion effects per protocol
  • Propofol infusion syndrome risk with prolonged/high-dose exposure

Onset and Duration

  • Onset: 15 to 45 seconds
  • Peak effect: about 1 minute
  • Duration (single bolus): 5 to 10 minutes
  • Context-sensitive half-time (CST 1/2): ~15 min at 2 hr infusion, ~20 min at 4 hr, ~30 min at 6 hr

Mechanism of Action

  • Positive allosteric modulator of GABA-A receptors (enhances inhibitory neurotransmission)

Contraindications / Avoid

  • Known hypersensitivity to propofol or formulation components
  • Hemodynamic instability where significant hypotension is high risk (relative)

Key Warnings and Cautions

  • Dose-dependent hypotension and respiratory depression/apnea; mean arterial pressure drops approximately 17% on induction in healthy adults
  • Pain on injection (consider lidocaine strategies per local practice)
  • Use caution in hypovolemia, poor cardiac reserve, elderly, frail
  • Propofol infusion syndrome risk with prolonged/high-dose infusions

Common Adverse Effects

  • Hypotension
  • Bradycardia
  • Apnea / hypoventilation
  • Injection site pain

Monitoring

  • Continuous SpO2, ECG, blood pressure
  • Airway/ventilation monitoring (capnography where available)
  • Hemodynamic response after each bolus or rate change

Important Interactions

  • Additive CNS/respiratory depression with opioids, benzodiazepines, volatile agents
  • Greater hypotension risk with other vasodilatory or negative inotropic drugs

Preparation and Administration Notes

  • Use strict aseptic technique (lipid emulsion)
  • Label syringe/bag with concentration and time prepared
  • Follow local policy for hang time/discard intervals

Reversal / Overdose Strategy

  • No direct reversal agent
  • Support airway, ventilation, oxygenation, and circulation
  • Reduce/stop infusion and treat hemodynamic instability

Special Populations

  • Elderly: typically lower dose requirements
  • Pediatrics: follow age-specific guidance
  • Obesity: dose strategy may vary by indication and institutional protocol

Documentation Checklist

  • Indication and target depth/sedation goal
  • Bolus and infusion doses with timestamps
  • Response, adverse effects, interventions
  • Ongoing reassessment and rationale for dose adjustments

Notes for Local Customization

  • Replace dose ranges with your institution's protocols where they differ
