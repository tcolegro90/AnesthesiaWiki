Comorbidities

Anesthetic considerations by patient condition

🧬 Acute Intermittent Porphyria (AIP) ▶

A genetic disorder caused by deficiency in the heme biosynthetic pathway. Certain anesthetic agents can provoke a dangerous acute porphyric crisis.

  • UNSAFE: Thiopental, thiamylal, methohexital (barbiturates) are absolutely contraindicated.
  • UNSAFE: Etomidate, ketamine, ketorolac, amiodarone, and many calcium channel blockers.
  • SAFE: Propofol, dexmedetomidine, nitrous oxide, isoflurane, sevoflurane, desflurane, succinylcholine.
🌬️ Asthma / Reactive Airway Disease ▶

Patients with active bronchospasm or reactive airway disease are at heightened risk for severe respiratory events, often triggered by airway instrumentation.

  • Preferred: Propofol blunts airway reflexes effectively.
  • Preferred: Ketamine is an excellent IV induction choice due to bronchodilation.
  • Avoid: Histamine-releasing agents when possible (e.g., high-dose atracurium, morphine) because they can worsen bronchospasm.
🔥 Burn Injuries ▶

Thermal injuries cause major physiologic shifts, including up-regulation of extrajunctional acetylcholine receptors.

  • Contraindicated: Succinylcholine from 24-48 hours post-burn through healing due to severe hyperkalemia risk.
  • Preferred paralytic: Nondepolarizing NMBAs (e.g., rocuronium), often at higher doses.
  • Anticipate airway edema and potential difficult airway.
Cardiovascular Disease ▶

Coronary disease, heart failure, and conduction disease change tolerance for rapid hemodynamic shifts and many vasoactive/antiarrhythmic drugs.

  • Caution: Rapid beta blockade (esmolol/labetalol) can precipitate bradycardia or low output in vulnerable patients.
  • Caution: Vasodilators (nitroglycerin, nitroprusside, nicardipine) can cause hypotension and ischemia if over-titrated.
  • Caution: LAST treatment uses modified ACLS with low-dose epinephrine and avoidance of vasopressin.
Chronic Kidney Disease ▶

Renal dysfunction can prolong or intensify effects of multiple anesthetic and perioperative medications.

  • Avoid: NSAIDs such as ketorolac in advanced renal dysfunction when possible.
  • Caution: Sugammadex and some antihypertensives may require renal-focused risk assessment.
  • Caution: Titrate vasodilators and monitor perfusion closely to avoid renal hypoperfusion.
Diabetes Mellitus ▶

Glucose control and autonomic variability can affect perioperative management and medication response.

  • Caution: Dexamethasone can raise glucose and may warrant tighter perioperative monitoring.
  • Caution: Avoid prolonged fasting and maintain glucose strategy tailored to insulin use.
  • Preferred: Use multimodal antiemetics/analgesia to reduce stress response and opioid burden.
🧩 Down Syndrome (Trisomy 21) ▶

Physical and structural features require careful airway and positioning planning.

  • Airway: Macroglossia can complicate mask ventilation and intubation.
  • Critical: Atlantoaxial instability makes neck flexion/extension hazardous.
  • Higher incidence of congenital heart disease and subglottic stenosis.
Hepatic Dysfunction ▶

Liver dysfunction can reduce clearance and increase exposure for many anesthetic and adjunctive medications.

  • Caution: Consider lower or spaced dosing for hepatically cleared agents such as midazolam and vecuronium.
  • Avoid: Excess acetaminophen total daily exposure in active liver disease.
  • Caution: Monitor for prolonged sedation or delayed emergence.
⚡ Mitochondrial Diseases ▶

Mitochondrial disorders impair cellular energy metabolism and can alter anesthetic drug responses.

  • Consider: Increased sensitivity to anesthetic dose and metabolic stress.
  • Caution: Propofol infusions may increase risk of propofol infusion syndrome.
  • Caution: Volatile anesthetics may further impair mitochondrial function.
  • Avoid prolonged fasting and maintain glucose support when appropriate.
💪 Muscular Dystrophy & Neuromuscular Disorders ▶

Patients with muscular dystrophy or certain channelopathies have major risk with specific neuromuscular and anesthetic agents.

  • Contraindicated: Succinylcholine due to hyperkalemia and arrhythmia risk.
  • Caution: Volatile agents in patients with MH susceptibility (e.g., RYR1 mutation).
  • Prefer TIVA and nondepolarizing neuromuscular blockers with quantitative monitoring and planned reversal.
Narrow-Angle Glaucoma ▶

Anticholinergic medications can worsen angle closure and increase intraocular pressure risk.

  • Avoid: Scopolamine patch in known narrow-angle glaucoma.
  • Caution: Other anticholinergic agents should be selected carefully in susceptible patients.
⚖️ Obesity ▶

Obesity significantly changes respiratory physiology, airway risk, and pharmacokinetics.

  • Consider: Dose most agents using IBW/LBW; TBW is used for selected drugs.
  • Consider: Altered inhaled anesthetic handling and higher fluoride levels reported with sevoflurane.
  • Prepare for difficult airway, reduced FRC, and increased aspiration risk.
Pregnancy ▶

Pregnancy changes physiology, aspiration risk, and medication safety considerations.

  • Avoid: Ketorolac in labor/delivery settings and late pregnancy where contraindicated.
  • Caution: Use blood pressure agents based on obstetric protocol and fetal context.
  • Preferred: Use dose-aware multimodal plans with maternal-fetal monitoring.
