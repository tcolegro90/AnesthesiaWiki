# Dexamethasone

## Quick Facts
- Generic: Dexamethasone
- Brand: Decadron (and others)
- Class: Corticosteroid / antiemetic
- Controlled substance: No
- Last updated: 2026-04-04

## Indication-Specific Guidance

### 1) PONV Prophylaxis
#### Dosing
- **4–8 mg IV** at induction (not at end of case — works best when given early)
#### Practical Considerations
- Give **at or shortly after induction**, not at end of surgery (takes ~2 hours to reach peak effect)
- Effective as part of multimodal PONV prophylaxis (e.g., dexamethasone + ondansetron)
- Reduce dose to **4 mg** when combined with aprepitant (due to CYP3A4 interaction)

### 2) Airway Edema (Croup / Post-Extubation)
#### Dosing
- 0.5–1 mg/kg IV (max 10–16 mg)
#### Practical Considerations
- Consider in patients at risk for post-extubation stridor (prolonged intubation, pediatrics, cuff-leak concern)

## Onset and Duration
- Onset: IV — approximately 2 hours to peak antiemetic effect
- Duration: 24–48 hours

## Mechanism of Action
- Blood-brain barrier penetration
- Exact antiemetic mechanism unclear — may involve prostaglandin inhibition and serotonin inhibition at solitary tract nucleus
- Anti-inflammatory and anti-edema effects (useful for airway applications)

## Contraindications / Avoid
- Active systemic fungal infection
- Live vaccine administration within 2 weeks
- Hypersensitivity to dexamethasone or any component

## Key Warnings and Cautions
- **Hyperglycemia**: Transient blood glucose elevation is common — a single 4–8 mg dose rarely causes harmful hyperglycemia in non-diabetic patients, but **monitor glucose in diabetics** (especially IDDM/T1DM)
- Immunosuppression — typically not clinically significant with a single perioperative dose
- In immunocompromised patients or patients with poorly controlled diabetes, weigh risk/benefit

## Common Adverse Effects
- Transient hyperglycemia
- Facial flushing (particularly with rapid IV injection)
- Perineal burning/tingling with rapid IV administration — inject slowly

## Monitoring
- Blood glucose (diabetic patients)

## Important Interactions
- **Aprepitant (NK₁ antagonist)**: CYP3A4 inhibition increases dexamethasone levels — use 4 mg instead of 8 mg when combined with aprepitant

## Notes for Local Customization
- Often combined with ondansetron as first-line PONV prophylaxis (complementary mechanisms, additive effect).
