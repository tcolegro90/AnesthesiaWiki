Scopolamine (Transderm-Scop)

Quick Facts

  • Generic: Scopolamine
  • Brand: Transderm-Scop (patch), Scopace (oral)
  • Class: Anticholinergic / Muscarinic antagonist
  • Controlled substance: No
  • Last updated: 2026-04-04

Indication-Specific Guidance

1) PONV Prophylaxis (Transdermal Patch)

Dosing

  • 1 patch (1.5 mg / 2.5 cm²) applied behind the ear at least 2–4 hours before surgery (ideally night before)

Practical Considerations

  • Long duration of release (~72 hours) makes it highly effective for post-discharge nausea and vomiting (PDNV) — a key advantage over shorter-acting agents
  • Patient and staff must be counseled: wash hands after handling; do not touch eyes (risk of mydriasis)

2) Motion Sickness Prevention

Dosing

  • Same patch; apply night before travel

Onset and Duration

  • Onset: 4 hours (transdermal; must apply well before surgery)
  • Duration: up to 72 hours (continuous release)
  • 1.5 mg total drug per patch

Mechanism of Action

  • Anticholinergic — competitive antagonism of muscarinic (M₁) receptors in the vestibular system and vomiting center; reduces vestibular and vagal afferent input to the CTZ

Contraindications / Avoid

  • Glaucoma (especially narrow-angle) — anticholinergic can precipitate acute angle-closure crisis
  • Urinary retention or BPH
  • Known hypersensitivity

Key Warnings and Cautions

  • Anticholinergic side effects: dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention, constipation, sedation
  • Mydriasis / photophobia: if drug contacts eyes — wash hands thoroughly after handling
  • Central anticholinergic syndrome: confusion, disorientation (especially elderly, pediatrics)
  • Elderly patients are particularly susceptible to CNS effects — use with caution

Common Adverse Effects

  • Dry mouth (most common)
  • Blurred vision
  • Drowsiness
  • Skin irritation at application site
  • Mydriasis (if eye contact)

Monitoring

  • No specific monitoring required in healthy adults
  • Mental status in elderly post-operatively

Notes for Local Customization

  • Most effective when applied night before or at least 4 hours before procedure. Reminder needed for same-day surgery patients.
