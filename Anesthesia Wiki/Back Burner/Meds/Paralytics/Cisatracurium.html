Cisatracurium (Nimbex)

Quick Facts

  • Generic: Cisatracurium besylate
  • Brand: Nimbex
  • Class: Nondepolarizing neuromuscular blocking agent (NDMRA; benzylisoquinolinium)
  • Controlled substance: No
  • Last updated: 2026-04-04

Indication-Specific Guidance

1) Facilitation of Intubation

Typical Dosing

  • 0.15 to 0.2 mg/kg IV — onset ~3–5 minutes

Practical Considerations

  • Slower onset than rocuronium; not ideal for RSI
  • Preferred when organ-independent elimination is desired (renal/hepatic failure)

2) ICU Paralysis / Prolonged Infusion

Typical Dosing

  • 1 to 3 mcg/kg/min continuous infusion; titrated to TOF monitoring

Practical Considerations

  • Drug of choice for prolonged paralysis in critically ill patients with organ dysfunction
  • Hofmann elimination is temperature and pH dependent (hypothermia slows elimination)

3) Maintenance Relaxation

Typical Dosing

  • 0.03 mg/kg supplemental boluses guided by TOF

Practical Considerations

  • Intermediate duration; plan reversal timing accordingly

Onset and Duration

  • Onset: 3 to 5 minutes (intermediate)
  • Duration: 40 to 75 minutes

Mechanism of Action

  • Competitive antagonist at nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nondepolarizing)
  • Undergoes Hofmann elimination: spontaneous non-enzymatic degradation at physiologic pH and temperature — independent of liver and kidney function

Contraindications / Avoid

  • Known hypersensitivity to cisatracurium or benzylisoquinolinium compounds

Key Warnings and Cautions

  • Cannot be reversed with sugammadex — does not bind the cyclodextrin. Must use neostigmine (at adequate spontaneous recovery) or allow full Hofmann elimination
  • No cardiovascular effects and no histamine release at clinically used doses — advantage over atracurium
  • Hofmann elimination can be slowed by hypothermia and acidosis

Common Adverse Effects

  • Prolonged block if organ dysfunction is more severe than anticipated
  • Rare hypersensitivity reactions

Monitoring

  • TOF monitoring required throughout use
  • Respiratory function assessment before extubation

Important Interactions

  • Volatile anesthetics: potentiate block
  • Magnesium: prolongs and intensifies block
  • Aminoglycosides, calcium channel blockers: may prolong block

Reversal / Overdose Strategy

  • Neostigmine + glycopyrrolate: effective at moderate block (TOF ≥ 2)
  • Allow full Hofmann elimination if block is too deep for neostigmine reversal
  • Sugammadex: NOT effective

Special Populations

  • Renal/hepatic failure: drug of choice — elimination is organ-independent
  • Burns: require higher doses due to up-regulated AChRs
  • Hypothermic patients: slower Hofmann elimination — monitor TOF more carefully

Notes for Local Customization

  • Preferred NDMRA in organ failure patients at many institutions.
