Mivacurium

Quick Facts

  • Generic: Mivacurium chloride
  • Class: Nondepolarizing neuromuscular blocking agent (NDMRA; benzylisoquinolinium)
  • Controlled substance: No
  • Last updated: 2026-04-04

Indication-Specific Guidance

1) Short Surgical Procedures / Intubation

Typical Dosing

  • 0.15 to 0.25 mg/kg IV

Practical Considerations

  • Shortest duration of all NDMRAs — useful for brief procedures
  • Slower onset than succinylcholine; not suitable for true RSI

Onset and Duration

  • Onset: 2 to 4 minutes
  • Duration: 12 to 20 minutes (shortest among NDMRAs)

Mechanism of Action

  • Competitive antagonist at nicotinic receptors (nondepolarizing)
  • Uniquely metabolized by plasma pseudocholinesterase (like succinylcholine)

Contraindications / Avoid

  • Pseudocholinesterase deficiency: Severely prolonged blockade — same enzyme as succinylcholine
  • Known hypersensitivity

Key Warnings and Cautions

  • Pseudocholinesterase deficiency: duration can extend to hours — treat like a phase II block
  • Histamine release: mild; inject slowly
  • May not be suitable for RSI due to slower onset

Common Adverse Effects

  • Flushing / mild histamine release
  • Prolonged block in pseudocholinesterase deficient patients

Monitoring

  • TOF monitoring
  • Screen for pseudocholinesterase deficiency history before use

Reversal / Overdose Strategy

  • Neostigmine: effective; short spontaneous duration often doesn't require reversal in standard patients
  • Sugammadex: NOT effective
  • Pseudocholinesterase deficiency: supportive — ventilate and wait

Notes for Local Customization

  • Less commonly available in modern US institutions; confirm formulary.
