# Bupivacaine

## Quick Facts
- Generic: Bupivacaine
- Class: Amide local anesthetic
- Typical concentrations: 0.25%, 0.5%, 0.75%
- Last updated: 2026-04-04

## Indication-Specific Guidance

### 1) Local Infiltration / Field Block
#### Typical Dosing
- Without epinephrine: up to about 2.5 mg/kg (max 175 mg)
- With epinephrine: up to about 3 mg/kg (max 225 mg)
#### Practical Considerations
- More cardiotoxic than many alternatives at high systemic levels.
- Slow, incremental injection with frequent aspiration is essential.

### 2) Peripheral Nerve Block
#### Typical Dosing
- Often 0.25% to 0.5% depending on block and duration goals.
- Keep total mg within caps and account for all local anesthetics used.
#### Practical Considerations
- Long duration benefit must be balanced against toxicity risk.

### 3) Neuraxial (Spinal/Epidural)
#### Typical Dosing
- Procedure specific and protocol driven.
- Do not extrapolate infiltration maxes directly to neuraxial practice.
#### Practical Considerations
- Follow institutional neuraxial concentration/volume standards.

## Onset and Duration
- Onset: intermediate
- Duration: long

## Contraindications / Avoid
- Known amide local anesthetic allergy (rare)
- Situations where prolonged motor block is undesirable (context dependent)

## Key Warnings and Cautions
- LAST risk with severe cardiovascular toxicity profile
- Strict cumulative dosing and interval awareness needed

## Monitoring
- Standard monitoring for large-dose regional anesthesia
- Immediate readiness for LAST treatment pathway

## Notes for Local Customization
- Keep block-specific dosing tables per institutional protocol.
- Include lipid rescue access and workflow wherever high-volume regional anesthesia is performed.
