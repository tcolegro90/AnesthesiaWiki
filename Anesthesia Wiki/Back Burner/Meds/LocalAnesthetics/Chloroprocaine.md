# Chloroprocaine

## Quick Facts
- Generic: Chloroprocaine
- Class: Ester local anesthetic
- Typical concentrations: 1.5%, 2%, 3%
- Last updated: 2026-04-04

## Indication-Specific Guidance

### 1) Local Infiltration / Short Procedures
#### Typical Dosing
- Common references: up to about 11 mg/kg (max around 800 mg)
#### Practical Considerations
- Very rapid onset and short duration can fit brief cases.

### 2) Epidural (short duration use cases)
#### Typical Dosing
- Protocol dependent by concentration and volume.
#### Practical Considerations
- Useful when shorter block duration is preferred.

### 3) Peripheral Nerve / Field Techniques
#### Typical Dosing
- Less common than amide agents in many workflows.
- Follow institution-specific concentration and total mg limits.
#### Practical Considerations
- Include in cumulative toxicity accounting when combined with other local anesthetics.

## Onset and Duration
- Onset: rapid
- Duration: short

## Contraindications / Avoid
- Known ester local anesthetic allergy

## Key Warnings and Cautions
- LAST still possible at high systemic exposure
- Formulations containing sodium bisulfate preservative are neurotoxic; low pH (~3) may cause tissue irritation
- Not recommended for IV regional (Bier block) or Subarachnoid Block (SAB) — use only preservative-free formulations epidurally

## Monitoring
- Standard monitoring for regional anesthesia dosing

## Notes for Local Customization
- Keep route-specific restrictions and preservative guidance visible in local protocol notes.
