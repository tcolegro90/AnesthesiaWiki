# Vasopressin

## Quick Facts
- Generic: Vasopressin
- Brand: Vasostrict
- Class: Non-catecholamine vasopressor (V1 agonist)
- Controlled substance: No
- Last updated: 2026-04-04

## Indication-Specific Guidance

### 1) Vasodilatory Shock Adjunct
#### Dosing
- 0.01 to 0.04 units/min IV infusion (fixed-dose common)

### 2) Refractory Hypotension / Post-CPB Vasoplegia
#### Dosing
- Institution-specific bolus/infusion approaches

## Onset and Duration
- Onset: minutes
- Duration: short to moderate while infusion is running

## Mechanism of Action
- V1 receptor agonism causes vascular smooth muscle constriction independent of adrenergic receptors

## Contraindications / Avoid
- Relative caution in mesenteric, peripheral, or coronary ischemia

## Key Warnings and Cautions
- Can reduce perfusion to skin/gut/extremities at excessive doses
- Less chronotropic effect than catecholamines

## Common Adverse Effects
- Hypertension
- Ischemia (digital/mesenteric) at high doses
- Hyponatremia with prolonged use (antidiuretic activity)

## Monitoring
- MAP and perfusion markers
- Electrolytes if prolonged infusion

## Important Interactions
- Additive vasoconstriction with other vasopressors

## Notes for Local Customization
- Common adjunct when escalating catecholamine requirements in vasodilatory shock.
