# Epinephrine

## Quick Facts
- Generic: Epinephrine
- Brand: Adrenalin (and others)
- Class: Endogenous catecholamine / mixed alpha and beta agonist
- Controlled substance: No
- Last updated: 2026-04-04

## Indication-Specific Guidance

### 1) Perioperative Hypotension / Vasopressor Support
#### Dosing
- Bolus: 5 to 20 mcg IV for hypotension (titrate to response)
- Infusion: 0.01 to 0.2 mcg/kg/min IV

### 2) Anaphylaxis
#### Dosing
- IM: 0.3 to 0.5 mg (1 mg/mL) lateral thigh, repeat every 5–15 minutes
- Severe/anesthesia setting: titrated IV bolus or infusion per institutional protocol

### 3) Cardiac Arrest (ACLS)
#### Dosing
- 1 mg IV/IO every 3–5 minutes

## Onset and Duration
- Onset: rapid (seconds IV)
- Duration: short (minutes)

## Mechanism of Action
- Potent direct **alpha-1, alpha-2, beta-1, and beta-2 agonist**

## Contraindications / Avoid
- No absolute contraindication in life-threatening indications (anaphylaxis/arrest)
- Relative caution in severe ischemic heart disease and tachyarrhythmias

## Key Warnings and Cautions
- Can precipitate tachyarrhythmias and myocardial ischemia
- Extravasation risk with peripheral concentrated infusions

## Common Adverse Effects
- Tachycardia
- Hypertension
- Arrhythmias
- Hyperglycemia/lactic acidosis

## Monitoring
- Continuous ECG and BP
- Perfusion markers and lactate in shock states

## Important Interactions
- Potentiation with MAO inhibitors, tricyclics, cocaine/sympathomimetics

## Notes for Local Customization
- Legacy wiki described epinephrine as a potent direct alpha and beta agonist.
