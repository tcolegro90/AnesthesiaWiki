# Sugammadex (Bridion)

## Quick Facts
- Generic: Sugammadex sodium
- Brand: Bridion
- Class: Selective relaxant binding agent (modified gamma-cyclodextrin)
- Controlled substance: No
- Last updated: 2026-04-04

## Indication-Specific Guidance

### 1) Reversal of Moderate Rocuronium / Vecuronium Block (TOF ≥ 2)
#### Typical Dosing
- 2 mg/kg IV
#### Practical Considerations
- Achieves reversal in ~3 minutes
- Check TOF count before dosing

### 2) Reversal of Deep Block (TOF = 1, or post-tetanic count 1–2)
#### Typical Dosing
- 4 mg/kg IV
#### Practical Considerations
- Still significantly faster than waiting for spontaneous recovery

### 3) Immediate Reversal of RSI Dose Rocuronium (1.2 mg/kg, "Can't Intubate Can't Oxygenate")
#### Typical Dosing
- 16 mg/kg IV — immediate (within minutes) reversal of RSI-dose rocuronium
#### Practical Considerations
- Life-saving in CICO emergency when rocuronium was used for RSI and intubation failed
- This is the primary reason high-dose rocuronium as a succinylcholine alternative requires real-time sugammadex access

## Onset and Duration
- Onset: 1 to 2 minutes (dose dependent — faster at higher doses)
- Duration of effect: 2 to 16 hours (sufficient for full wear-off)

## Mechanism of Action
- Encapsulates (chelates) steroidal NMBAs (rocuronium >> vecuronium) within its cavity, forming a tight 1:1 complex that is pharmacologically inert and renally excreted
- **Only works on aminosteroid NMBAs** (rocuronium and vecuronium) — not effective for cisatracurium, atracurium, succinylcholine, or other classes

## Contraindications / Avoid
- Known hypersensitivity to sugammadex
- **Does NOT reverse**: succinylcholine, cisatracurium, atracurium, mivacurium

## Key Warnings and Cautions
- Anaphylaxis / hypersensitivity: reported — have resuscitation equipment available
- Bradycardia and cardiac arrest: rare but reported; monitor during administration
- **Recurarization**: Can theoretically occur if sugammadex dosing is inadequate; verify TOF recovery
- Hormonal contraceptive interaction: sugammadex binds progestin-containing contraceptives, potentially reducing efficacy for up to 7 days. Advise backup contraception.
- Renal impairment: the sugammadex-NMBA complex is eliminated renally; caution in severe renal failure (accumulation)

## Common Adverse Effects
- Dysgeusia (metallic/bitter taste)
- Bradycardia
- Hypotension (rare)
- Hypersensitivity reactions

## Monitoring
- TOF monitoring before and after administration
- Heart rate and blood pressure monitoring during bolus

## Important Interactions
- Toremifene and flucloxacillin: may compete for binding; dose suggestion may need adjustment
- Oral contraceptives: reduced efficacy (see Warnings above)

## Preparation and Administration Notes
- Administer as a single rapid IV bolus over 10 seconds
- Use within 5 minutes of drawing; discard unused portion

## Reversal / Overdose Strategy
- If re-paralysis is needed after sugammadex reversal, use benzylisoquinolinium NMBAs (e.g., cisatracurium) or wait approximately 24 hours before using rocuronium again

## Special Populations
- Renal insufficiency: use with caution; avoid if GFR < 30 (creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min) per labeling
- Pregnancy: limited data; use only if clearly needed

## Notes for Local Customization
- Verify institutional protocol for post-reversal monitoring duration requirements.
