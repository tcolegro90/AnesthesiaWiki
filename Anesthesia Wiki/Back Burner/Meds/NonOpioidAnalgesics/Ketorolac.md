# Ketorolac

## Quick Facts
- Generic: Ketorolac
- Brand: Toradol
- Class: NSAID (nonselective COX inhibitor)
- Controlled substance: No
- Last updated: 2026-04-04

## Indication-Specific Guidance

### 1) Perioperative Analgesia (Opioid-Sparing)
#### Dosing
- Standard adult IV dose: 30 mg once
- Use 15 mg in older adults, renal impairment, or body weight < 50 kg
#### Practical Considerations
- Most useful when bleeding risk is low and renal function is adequate
- Can reduce postoperative opioid requirement

## Onset and Duration
- Onset: 5-10 minutes IV
- Peak: 1-2 hours
- Duration: 4-6 hours analgesia

## Mechanism of Action
- Inhibits cyclooxygenase (COX-1 and COX-2), reducing prostaglandin synthesis and nociceptive inflammation

## Contraindications / Avoid
- Active peptic ulcer disease or GI bleeding
- Advanced renal failure
- Significant active bleeding/coagulopathy
- Labor and delivery
- NSAID hypersensitivity

## Key Warnings and Cautions
- Increases risk of bleeding and renal injury in susceptible patients
- Use shortest duration possible

## Common Adverse Effects
- GI irritation
- Bleeding tendency
- Renal dysfunction

## Monitoring
- Renal function in at-risk patients
- Signs of bleeding

## Important Interactions
- Additive bleeding with anticoagulants/antiplatelets
- Increased renal risk with nephrotoxic agents
