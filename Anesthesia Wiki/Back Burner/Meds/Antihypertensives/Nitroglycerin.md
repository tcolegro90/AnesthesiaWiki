# Nitroglycerin (NTG)

## Quick Facts
- Generic: Nitroglycerin
- Brand: Nitrostat (SL), Nitro-Bid (paste), Nitro-Dur (patch), Tridil (IV)
- Class: Organic nitrate / venodilator / coronary vasodilator
- Controlled substance: No
- Last updated: 2026-04-04

## Indication-Specific Guidance

### 1) Acute Hypertension / Hypertensive Urgency (IV)
#### Typical Dosing
- IV infusion: 20 to 200 mcg/min, titrated to effect
- IV bolus: 20 mcg incremental boluses for rapid transient effect
#### Practical Considerations
- Primarily venodilatory — greatest benefit when preload reduction is the goal
- Less arterial vasodilation than hydralazine or nicardipine at lower doses

### 2) Myocardial Ischemia / Angina Relief
#### Typical Dosing
- Sublingual: 0.4 mg (400 mcg), repeat every 5 minutes up to 3 doses
- IV infusion: start 5 mcg/min, titrate upward by 5–10 mcg/min every 3–5 minutes
#### Practical Considerations
- Increases coronary blood flow by dilating coronary vasculature

### 3) Uterine Relaxation (Tocolysis, Placenta Delivery)
#### Typical Dosing
- 50 to 100 mcg IV or sublingual bolus
#### Practical Considerations
- Off-label but commonly used by anesthesia providers to relax the uterus for placenta delivery or external version

### 4) Nitro-Paste (Topical)
#### Typical Dosing
- 0.5 to 2 inches applied to skin
#### Practical Considerations
- Slower absorption; used for longer-term prophylaxis

## Onset and Duration
- IV: onset 1 to 2 minutes; duration 3 to 5 minutes (infusion)
- Sublingual: onset 1 to 3 minutes; duration 25 to 30 minutes

## Mechanism of Action
- **Pure venous vasodilator** — donates nitric oxide (NO), activating guanylate cyclase → increased cGMP → venous smooth muscle relaxation
- Dilates capacitance veins, pooling blood peripherally, which **decreases venous return and preload**, reducing myocardial oxygen demand
- At higher doses, some arterial dilation also occurs

## Contraindications / Avoid
- Concomitant use of **PDE-5 inhibitors** (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil): severe, potentially fatal hypotension — absolutely contraindicated within 24–48 hours (depending on agent)
- Severe hypotension or hypovolemia
- Right ventricular infarction (preload-dependent — NTG can cause severe hypotension)
- Closed-angle glaucoma (theoretical: increased intraocular pressure concern — debated)

## Key Warnings and Cautions
- **Reflex tachycardia**: Venous pooling drops preload and BP → sympathetic reflex → tachycardia
- Tolerance develops with continuous infusion (requires nitrate-free interval)
- Hypotension if patient is hypovolemic

## Common Adverse Effects
- Headache (most common — vasodilation of meningeal vessels)
- Tachycardia
- Hypotension
- Dizziness / flushing

## Monitoring
- Blood pressure and heart rate during infusion (every 1–5 minutes depending on acuity)

## Important Interactions
- **PDE-5 inhibitors**: fatal hypotension risk — screen all patients

## Notes for Local Customization
- Clinical rule: Hydralazine = pure arterial dilator; Nitroglycerin = pure venous dilator.
