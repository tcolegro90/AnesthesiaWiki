Chin Lift vs Jaw Thrust vs Larson Maneuver

Three core airway rescue maneuvers with distinct goals: opening a soft tissue-obstructed airway, minimizing cervical movement, and breaking laryngospasm.

Quick Comparison

Maneuver Primary Use Key Technique Avoid / Caution
Chin Lift Basic airway opening in reduced consciousness with tongue-related obstruction. One hand on forehead to tilt, fingertips under bony chin to lift the mandible upward. Use caution with known or suspected C-spine injury due to neck extension.
Jaw Thrust Airway opening when cervical spine motion should be minimized. Place fingers behind mandibular angles and displace jaw anteriorly while keeping head neutral. Can be painful in lightly sedated patients; consider additional anesthesia/analgesia if appropriate.
Larson Maneuver Breaking laryngospasm (often with 100% O2, CPAP, and deepened anesthesia). Firm bilateral pressure at laryngospasm notch behind earlobe near mastoid while performing jaw thrust. Do not delay definitive treatment: deepen anesthesia and give succinylcholine if persistent.

When to Reach for Each

  1. Simple obstruction, no trauma concern: start with chin lift plus mask repositioning.
  2. Possible C-spine injury: prioritize jaw thrust with manual in-line stabilization.
  3. Suspected laryngospasm: apply Larson maneuver while giving 100% oxygen and CPAP.
  4. No rapid improvement: escalate early to pharmacologic treatment and advanced airway steps.
Practical sequence for laryngospasm: remove stimulus, call for help, 100% O2 + tight seal + CPAP, jaw thrust/Larson, deepen anesthesia, then paralysis/intubation if unresolved.

Reference Notes

  • Larson maneuver is an adjunct, not a replacement for oxygenation and timely pharmacologic escalation.
  • In pediatrics, complete airway obstruction can desaturate rapidly; prioritize early coordinated intervention.
  • Use local institutional difficult airway and laryngospasm protocols as definitive guidance.
