Indications for a Smaller ETT
  • 🔥
    Airway Edema / Swelling — Expected due to trauma, burns, anaphylaxis, or prolonged prone/Trendelenburg positioning.
  • ⚠️
    Known Stenosis — Subglottic stenosis, tracheal strictures, or vocal cord pathology.
  • 🔬
    Surgical Requirements — Shared airway (ENT, vocal cord microsurgery) where the surgeon needs maximal glottic visualization.
  • ⏱️
    Anticipated Prolonged Intubation — Smaller tube minimizes mucosal pressure and reduces post-extubation stenosis risk.
  • 📏
    Patient Stature — Exceptionally short or small-framed adults.

📹 Video Laryngoscopy — Indications▼
  • 😮
    Anticipated Difficult Airway — High Mallampati score, obesity, short neck, or history of previous difficult intubation.
  • 🦴
    Cervical Spine Instability — VL requires significantly less neck manipulation and extension than direct laryngoscopy.
  • ↔️
    Limited Mouth Opening — Hyper-angulated blades are easier to insert in patients with restricted inter-incisor gap.
  • 🔄
    Poor DL View — Rescue device when initial direct laryngoscopy yields a Grade III or IV Cormack-Lehane view.
  • 🎓
    Teaching / Supervision — Allows the attending to see exactly what the intubator sees on the screen in real time.

🧪 Airway Assessment Tools▼

These bedside airway assessment tools help estimate difficulty with mask ventilation, laryngoscopy, and intubation. No single tool is definitive, so combine findings rather than relying on one data point.

Tool How to Assess What Raises Concern
Mallampati Patient seated, mouth fully open, tongue protruded without phonation; classify I-IV. Class III-IV suggests reduced direct line-of-sight to glottic structures.
Thyromental Distance Measure from mentum to thyroid notch with neck extended. Short distance (commonly < 6 cm or roughly < 3 fingerbreadths) may predict difficult laryngoscopy.
Interincisor Distance Measure maximal mouth opening between upper and lower incisors. Limited opening (often < 3-4 cm) can restrict laryngoscope and tube passage.
Mandibular Protrusion Upper lip bite test (ability of lower incisors to bite upper lip). Poor mandibular advancement (worse ULBT class) correlates with harder laryngoscopic view.
Atlanto-occipital Mobility Assess neck extension/flexion and ability to align oral-pharyngeal-laryngeal axes. Limited mobility can reduce glottic exposure and increase need for alternative devices.
Dentition Inspect loose teeth, dentures, veneers/crowns, and prominent incisors. Fragile/prosthetic teeth increase trauma risk and can complicate blade insertion and mask seal.

🫁 Chin Lift vs Jaw Thrust vs Larson Maneuver▼
Maneuver Primary Use Technique Cautions
Chin Lift Open upper airway in reduced consciousness when tongue obstruction is likely. Head tilt with one hand, lift bony chin with fingertips to move tongue off posterior pharynx. Use caution if possible C-spine injury due to neck extension.
Jaw Thrust Open airway with minimal cervical movement. Place fingers behind mandibular angles and displace jaw anteriorly while keeping head neutral. Can be painful in light anesthesia; optimize sedation/analgesia as needed.
Larson Maneuver Adjunct to break laryngospasm. Firm bilateral pressure at laryngospasm notch while maintaining jaw thrust and tight mask seal. Do not delay escalation to deepening anesthesia and succinylcholine if unresolved.
Suggested Escalation Flow for Suspected Laryngospasm
  • 1
    Remove trigger and call for help — stop stimulation and optimize position.
  • 2
    100% O2 + CPAP + Larson manuever / jaw thrust — ensure a tight mask seal and apply Larson maneuver.
  • 3
    Deepen anesthesia — if not rapidly improving.
  • 4
    Paralyze/intubate early when needed — if persistent obstruction or desaturation.
