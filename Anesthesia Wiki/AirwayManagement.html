Indications for a Smaller ETT

  • Airway Edema / Swelling — Expected due to trauma, burns, anaphylaxis, or prolonged prone/Trendelenburg positioning.
  • Known Stenosis — Subglottic stenosis, tracheal strictures, or vocal cord pathology.
  • Surgical Requirements — Shared airway surgeries (ENT, vocal cord microsurgery) where the surgeon needs maximal glottic visualization.
  • Anticipated Prolonged Intubation — Smaller tube minimizes tracheal mucosal pressure and reduces post-extubation stenosis risk.
  • Patient Stature — Exceptionally short or small-framed adults.

Video Laryngoscopy (VL) — Indications
  • Anticipated Difficult Airway — High Mallampati score, high BMI/obesity, or history of previous difficult intubation.
  • Cervical Spine Instability — VL requires significantly less neck manipulation and extension to obtain a glottic view compared to DL.
  • Limited Mouth Opening — Hyper-angulated VL blades are easier to insert in patients with restricted inter-incisor gaps.
  • Poor DL View — Rescue device when initial DL attempts result in a Grade III or IV Cormack-Lehane view.
  • Teaching / Supervision — Allows the attending or preceptor to see exactly what the intubator is seeing on the screen.
