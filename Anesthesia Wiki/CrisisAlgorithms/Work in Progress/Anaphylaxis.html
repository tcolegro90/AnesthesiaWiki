Emergency Algorithms

Anaphylaxis

Example high-speed OR crisis layout for review. If this format looks right, I will apply it to all remaining crisis topics.

Example Format - Review First

First 60 Seconds

  • Call for help and announce suspected anaphylaxis.
  • Stop potential trigger exposure if possible.
  • 100% oxygen, secure airway, and support ventilation.
  • Start epinephrine promptly for hypotension, bronchospasm, or airway edema.
  • Begin rapid IV fluid bolus and prepare vasopressor infusion if unstable.

Recognition Clues

  • Sudden hypotension after drug, antiseptic, latex, or blood product exposure.
  • Rising peak airway pressures, wheeze, bronchospasm, poor ventilation.
  • Cutaneous signs: flushing, urticaria, angioedema (may be absent under drapes).
  • Cardiovascular collapse, tachycardia or bradycardia, pulselessness in severe cases.

Immediate Treatment Priorities

  • Epinephrine IV bolus, titrated to response in severe reaction.
  • Large-volume crystalloid resuscitation.
  • Adjuncts after epinephrine: antihistamine, steroid, bronchodilator.
  • Consider infusion support if repeated boluses are required.

Differential To Keep In Mind

  • Hemorrhage or occult major blood loss.
  • Tension pneumothorax, severe bronchospasm, or tube obstruction.
  • Pulmonary embolism, venous air embolism, high spinal, or MI.
  • Medication effect or overdose not related to allergy.

Stabilization And Aftercare

  • Draw serum tryptase per institutional workflow (initial and follow-up sample).
  • Document likely trigger timing and all meds given.
  • Place clear allergy warning in chart and handoff.
  • Plan monitored disposition (PACU/ICU) based on severity and recurrence risk.

Safety note: Use this as a rapid cognitive aid, and follow your local protocol/attending direction for exact dosing, escalation sequence, and post-event testing.
