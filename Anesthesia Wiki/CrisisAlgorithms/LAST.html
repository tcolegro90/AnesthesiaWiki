LAST

Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity

Immediate Priorities

  1. Stop local anesthetic injection immediately.
  2. Call for help and bring the LAST kit and 20% lipid emulsion.
  3. Airway and breathing: ventilate with 100% oxygen and avoid hypoxia, hypercarbia, and acidosis.
  4. Control seizures with benzodiazepines first-line; avoid large propofol doses in unstable patients.
  5. Start modified ACLS for cardiovascular instability or arrest.
Use lower epinephrine doses in LAST (prefer less than 1 mcg/kg per bolus). Avoid vasopressin and avoid calcium channel blockers and beta blockers during LAST resuscitation.

20% Lipid Emulsion Dosing

Step Dose
Initial bolus 1.5 mL/kg IV over about 1 minute
Continuous infusion 0.25 mL/kg/min
If unstable after initial response Repeat bolus 1.5 mL/kg (up to two additional boluses) and increase infusion to 0.5 mL/kg/min
Maximum dose Do not exceed approximately 12 mL/kg total lipid emulsion dose

Propofol is not a substitute for lipid emulsion therapy.

Post-Event Monitoring

  1. Continue close cardiopulmonary monitoring after stabilization.
  2. Monitor at least 4 to 6 hours after cardiovascular events.
  3. Monitor at least 2 hours after isolated, limited CNS symptoms.
  4. Consider early transfer to higher-level support if instability persists.

Common Early and Late Signs

Early signs can include perioral numbness, metallic taste, tinnitus, agitation, or confusion. Progression can include seizures, then cardiovascular changes such as hypotension, conduction abnormalities, ventricular arrhythmias, and cardiac arrest. Delayed and atypical presentations can occur.
