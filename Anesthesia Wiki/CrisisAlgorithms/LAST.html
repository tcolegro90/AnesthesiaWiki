LAST

1. Clinical Presentation

Perioral numbness
Metallic taste
Tinnitus
Agitation or confusion
Seizures
Hypotension / conduction changes / ventricular arrhythmias
Progression: CNS symptoms can precede cardiovascular collapse, but delayed or atypical presentations can occur.

2. Interventions

1
Stop local anesthetic injection immediately.
2
Call for help and bring the LAST kit and 20% lipid emulsion.
3
Manage airway and breathing with 100% oxygen. Avoid hypoxia, hypercarbia, and acidosis.
4
Control seizures with benzodiazepines first-line. Avoid large propofol doses in unstable patients.
5
Start modified ACLS for cardiovascular instability or arrest.
6
Use lower epinephrine doses. Prefer less than 1 mcg/kg per bolus.
Avoid: Vasopressin, calcium channel blockers, and beta blockers during LAST resuscitation. Propofol is not a substitute for lipid emulsion therapy.

20% Lipid Emulsion Dosing

  • Initial bolus: 1.5 mL/kg IV over about 1 minute.
  • Continuous infusion: 0.25 mL/kg/min.
  • If still unstable: Repeat 1.5 mL/kg bolus up to two more times and increase infusion to 0.5 mL/kg/min.
  • Maximum total dose: about 12 mL/kg.

Continue close cardiopulmonary monitoring after stabilization. Observe at least 4 to 6 hours after cardiovascular events, or at least 2 hours after isolated limited CNS symptoms.
