General Anesthesia ▶
You provided general anesthesia with no or minimal CRNA/physician supervision. COA requires documented cases across varying levels of supervision.
COA Guidelines — Supervision Levels
General anesthesia initiated with an IV agent (propofol, ketamine, etomidate, etc.). Most common induction technique in adult patients.
COA Guidelines — General Anesthesia
Gas induction without IV access — common in pediatrics, needle-phobic patients, or difficult IV access. ⚠️ Airway not secured until deeper anesthetic plane.
COA Guidelines — Pediatric & Special Techniques
Bag-mask ventilation during the induction phase before placement of an airway device. Count separately from mask maintenance.
COA Guidelines — Mask Ventilation
Entire maintenance phase delivered via mask airway — no LMA or ETT placed. Count separately from induction mask ventilation.
COA Guidelines — Mask Ventilation
Emergency bag-mask ventilation during a resuscitation or rescue event. Distinct from routine induction/maintenance mask use.
COA Guidelines — Crisis Management
Supraglottic airway device used for maintenance. COA requires supraglottic airway experience as part of airway management competency.
COA Guidelines — Supraglottic Airways
Other SGA devices — i-gel, King LT, Combitube, etc. Counts toward supraglottic airway experience alongside LMA cases.
COA Guidelines — Supraglottic Airways
Orotracheal intubation. COA minimum 35 intubations (oral + nasal combined). No simulation allowed toward the minimum.
COA Guidelines — Endotracheal Intubation (min. 35)
Nasotracheal intubation. Counts toward the 35-intubation minimum (combined oral + nasal). Requires nasal preparation and special technique considerations.
COA Guidelines — Endotracheal Intubation (min. 35)
Total intravenous anesthesia — no volatile agent used. Typically propofol infusion ± opioid ± ketamine. Used for PONV-sensitive patients and TIVA-only settings (e.g., MRI).
COA Guidelines — IV Anesthetic Techniques
You were responsible for managing emergence from anesthesia, including extubation and transition to recovery. COA requires documented emergence management.
COA Guidelines — Emergence Management
Regional Techniques ▶
Intrathecal injection. CSE counts as BOTH spinal AND epidural. Simulation allowed for part but not all required techniques.
COA Guidelines — Spinal (min. 10)
Epidural catheter or single-shot injection. CSE counts as BOTH epidural AND spinal. Can be for anesthesia or pain management.
COA Guidelines — Epidural (min. 10)
Upper/lower extremity or truncal blocks (TAP, rectus sheath, head/neck). No simulation for meeting the minimum requirement.
COA Guidelines — Peripheral Block (min. 10)
After meeting minimums for spinal, epidural, and peripheral, the remaining 5 required regional techniques can be any combination.
COA Guidelines — Additional Regional (5 remaining)
Managing an existing regional technique without performing the initial placement — e.g., topping up an in-situ epidural, adjusting a nerve block infusion. Counts toward regional experience.
COA Guidelines — Regional Techniques
Upper extremity peripheral nerve block — brachial plexus (interscalene, supraclavicular, infraclavicular, axillary) or distal UE blocks.
COA Guidelines — Peripheral Block (Upper)
Lower extremity peripheral nerve block — femoral, popliteal sciatic, adductor canal, iPACK, ankle block, or truncal (TAP, rectus sheath, QL).
COA Guidelines — Peripheral Block (Lower)
Monitored Anesthesia Care (MAC)
Anesthesia provider present throughout, providing sedation and/or analgesia. Patient maintains protective airway reflexes. ⚠️ Must be prepared to convert to general anesthesia if conditions change.
COA Guidelines — MAC
Moderate / Deep Sedation
Moderate: patient responds purposefully to verbal commands. Deep: not easily aroused but responds to stimulation. Provider monitors and manages airway, breathing, and circulation throughout.
COA Guidelines — Sedation
Case administered without any pharmacological anesthetic agents — supplemental O₂ and positioning only. Rare; typically a pure MAC or sedation-free case.
COA Guidelines — Medications
Volatile anesthetic agents — sevoflurane, desflurane, isoflurane — or nitrous oxide. Used for inhalation maintenance in most GETA cases.
COA Guidelines — Inhalation Agents
IV induction agents — propofol, ketamine, etomidate, midazolam, barbiturates. Document the primary induction agent(s) used.
COA Guidelines — IV Anesthetic Agents
Neuromuscular blocking agents (NMBAs) — succinylcholine, rocuronium, vecuronium, cisatracurium. ⚠️ Document reversal agent used (neostigmine, sugammadex).
COA Guidelines — Neuromuscular Blockade
IV opioid analgesics — fentanyl, morphine, hydromorphone, remifentanil, sufentanil, meperidine. Check all that were administered perioperatively.
COA Guidelines — Opioid Analgesics
Other IV agents — dexmedetomidine, ketamine (sub-anesthetic dose), systemic lidocaine, vasoactives, benzodiazepines, antiemetics.
COA Guidelines — Adjunct Agents
